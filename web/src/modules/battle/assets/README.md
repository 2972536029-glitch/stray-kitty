# Battle floor material atlas

`floor-material-atlas.png` is an original atlas generated with the built-in imagegen tool on 2026-10-27 for this project. It is used as floor texture only; gameplay geometry, props, characters and overlays remain in Canvas code. No third-party reference artwork is copied into the asset.

The image is a 3-column × 2-row atlas. Row 1: prologue wet slate, first-chapter damp street, dry warm underground market. Row 2: old greenhouse tiles, damp underground drain bricks, dry warehouse concrete. Each panel is sampled as 4 × 4 terrain pieces with nearest-neighbor filtering. All chapter masks and interaction coordinates stay unchanged. Loading failure falls back to deterministic procedural tiles.

## Scenery detail pass · 2026-10-30

The six battle maps retain this atlas and use the existing main-branch exploration art as their palette and material reference. `scene-art.ts` adds restrained, deterministic joint wear, moisture, glass fragments and expansion seams; these accents also render after the atlas loads. `scene-props.ts` draws the sixteen shared prop types with stepped silhouettes, contact shadows, material shading and small hardware details in the original 80 × 80 space.

`battle-scene-backdrop-art.ts` gives each chapter its own walls, windows, beams, lamps and perimeter materials. The backdrop renders at one backing pixel per CSS pixel. Its scenery mask excludes only visible combat cells within the board scrollport, leaving map holes available for decoration. Both the board and its scrollport are observed so zooming, scrolling and responsive layout do not leave stale masks. Names, spawn labels and meters remain DOM overlays; terrain and props contain no gameplay labels or route markings.

`scene-vignettes.ts` combines the same materials into six small environmental groups: seedling benches, potting corners, alley supplies, market goods, pump maintenance areas and warehouse workbenches, with restrained local traces of soil, moisture or wear. `scene-composition.ts` gives each chapter an authored asymmetric arrangement: each workstation appears once, with two different activities at different heights and scales in the greenhouse. A few smaller accents replace the former equally spaced rows; map holes are no longer automatically filled with duplicate furniture. Complete groups avoid combat cells and HUD bounds, and narrow screens omit them. Window damage, glazing, lamps and vines have deterministic variations rather than appearing identically in every bay. These are noninteractive background details and do not change obstacle or deployment rules.

## Continuous terrain and new environmental props · 2026-10-30

The board and its surrounding floor now use the same chapter atlas, tile scale and world origin. The backdrop follows board scrolling and zooming; native 80 px ground samples (100 px for the prologue) preserve the foreground's pixel density. The surrounding ground receives a quiet chapter-colored wash, while playable cells keep fine paving joints and their existing tactical highlights. Exposed map edges are worn ground seams instead of raised platform borders. Map geometry, tutorial logic, deployment rules and skill feedback are unchanged.

`scene-new-props-atlas.png` is an original transparent 4 × 2 atlas generated using the built-in imagegen tool for this project. It adds eight distinct silhouettes: a bicycle, a hydrant and hose, a covered tea/repair cart, hanging fabric bundles and baskets, a garden wheelbarrow, a water barrel, an electrical maintenance cabinet and cable reel, and a pallet jack. `scene-new-props-atlas.prompt.txt` records both generation and packing-correction prompts; `scene-new-props-atlas.json` records measured sprite bounds and transparency checks. No external reference artwork was used. The source image is retained without pixel editing.

`scene-new-props.ts` crops these sprites and renders them with nearest-neighbor filtering. Each chapter has a small authored selection at asymmetric positions. Complete objects avoid visible combat cells, HUD panels and one another; cramped views omit decorations rather than covering gameplay. These props are noninteractive scenery, with no game labels or route marks baked into the artwork.

## Original floor generation prompt

Use case: stylized-concept. Asset type: one production floor-material atlas for a top-down pixel-art cat tower-defense game. Generate ONE rectangular atlas with exactly 3 equal columns and 2 equal rows, six edge-to-edge rectangular swatches, no gutters, no border, no captions, no text. Pixel art with small crisp square pixel clusters, painted limited color shading and chipped material details, handcrafted 16-bit RPG environment quality. Camera perfectly overhead, flat ground; NOT an isometric room, NOT a perspective scene. Each panel is entirely floor material all the way to its panel edges. No characters, furniture, walls, lamps, plants standing above the surface, tactical grid, interface, arrows or readable marks. The game will overlay its own figures and gameplay grid. Textures need quiet contrast and interesting small-scale irregularity, avoid bold macro shapes. Keep all six stylistically consistent but environment and color completely different. Top left: cold slate blue-grey small irregular stone pavers with occasional shallow rain sheen, a rainy unfamiliar alley. Top middle: muted blue-grey weathered street pavers after rain, some dry stone and only restrained damp seams, not an actively raining scene. Top right: underground midnight market, warm ochre-grey worn flagstones and limestone slabs, dusty dry paths, sparse tiny bottle-cap sized metallic flecks, no water or rain. Bottom left: abandoned glass greenhouse, sage grey and faded terracotta tiles, worn mineral patches, fine moss only at joints and subtle pale diffuse skylight, no heavy rainfall, no dramatic day/night mood. Bottom middle: underground drainage tunnel, dark muted grey-green old brick paving, fine lime deposits, rust flecks, damp grout and small restrained seepage, no sky or rain. Bottom right: abandoned warehouse, dry charcoal-warm grey concrete slabs, subtle dust and hairline cracks with occasional metal expansion joints, no moss, no water, no rain. Palette sophisticated muted earthy colors, midtones readable underneath game characters, no pure black patches. Each panel should show many small stones/tiles (about 10-15 across) not gigantic blocks. Landscape atlas aspect 3:2, highest detailed pixel texture quality.
